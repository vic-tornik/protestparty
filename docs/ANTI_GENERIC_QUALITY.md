# Human-authored quality: avoiding generic or “AI-slop” signals

## 1. A necessary distinction

There is no dependable public test that can prove a Roblox game was or was not made with AI. Players usually react to the experience they can observe: originality, coherence, polish, responsiveness, writing quality, and whether the mechanics feel intentional. Do not optimize to conceal tool usage. Optimize for quality that is recognizably specific to this game.

AI can help draft code, ideate content, or generate concept art, but every shipped element still needs human direction, review, testing, and iteration.

## 2. Common low-quality signals to watch for

| Signal players may notice | Why it feels generic | How this project should fix it |
|---|---|---|
| Generic title and thumbnail | No identifiable premise | Use a coherent civic-nonsense visual identity and one memorable joke |
| Copy-pasted maps or free-model clutter | The space has no authored logic | Use a readable plaza layout; audit every external model and remove unused scripts |
| Randomly mixed art styles | Assets look assembled, not directed | Define a palette, shape language, material rules, lighting, and camera framing |
| Repetitive AI-sounding dialogue | Characters have no distinct voice | Give each NPC a role, motive, vocabulary, and short authored lines |
| Nonsensical reward economy | Numbers exist without purpose | Tie every reward to a visible unlock or progression goal |
| Huge empty map | Scale substitutes for gameplay | Make the active rally area dense and traversable; expand only when playtests justify it |
| Unclear UI and placeholder labels | No editorial pass | Establish hierarchy, consistent terminology, accessible contrast, and mobile-safe sizing |
| Identical quests with nouns swapped | Content volume without variation | Vary the player verb, timing, risk, social interaction, and outcome—not just the text |
| Fake depth through excessive particles | Visual noise without feedback | Use effects only to communicate cause, action, reward, or event state |
| Overused meme catchphrases | The game feels trend-chasing and dates quickly | Use original absurdist premises; rotate references in a curated catalog |
| Inconsistent difficulty and grind | No real balance tuning | Instrument action rates, round completion, reward curves, and player drop-offs |
| Bugs hidden by flashy visuals | Surface polish conceals weak foundations | Run structured tests, collect reports, and publish patch notes with real fixes |

## 3. Creative rules for PROTEST PARTY

1. **One joke per interaction.** A station, NPC, sign, or event should have one clear comedic premise.
2. **Commit to the bit.** The fake government treats ridiculous issues with serious process, forms, stamps, queues, and public notices.
3. **Use specific details.** Instead of “funny NPC,” write “a pigeon landlord who issues a rent invoice in crumbs.”
4. **Keep jokes readable at Roblox scale.** Short lines, clear silhouettes, large signs, and strong sound cues.
5. **Never make the player the joke in a hostile way.** Comedy should target fictional systems and minor inconveniences, not identity, disability, body, protected groups, or real victims.
6. **Do not build the entire game around a single viral trend.** The premise should still make sense after a meme phrase disappears.
7. **Make every reward understandable.** The player should know what action produced it and what it unlocks.
8. **Treat mobile as a first-class platform.** Controls, text size, tap targets, effects, and frame rate must work on modest devices.

## 4. Content editorial pipeline

Before adding a cause, NPC line, item name, emote, sound, or event:

- Is it original or properly licensed?
- Is the joke understandable without knowing a specific meme?
- Does it fit the fictional world and the same tone?
- Is it age-appropriate and aligned with Roblox Community Standards?
- Does it target a harmless situation rather than a real person or group?
- Does it add a distinct interaction or only inflate the catalog count?
- Can it be translated or shortened for mobile UI later?
- Has it been checked in context by a human editor and playtester?

## 5. Human playtest scorecard

Score each from 1 (weak) to 5 (excellent):

| Dimension | Test question |
|---|---|
| Identity | Could a player describe this game to a friend in one sentence? |
| First-minute clarity | Did the player know where to go and what to do? |
| Originality | Does the joke or interaction feel specific to this experience? |
| Coherence | Do UI, world, writing, sound, and rewards belong together? |
| Responsiveness | Do interactions give immediate, readable feedback? |
| Variety | Do two consecutive rounds feel meaningfully different? |
| Fairness | Can free players participate and progress meaningfully? |
| Social value | Is there a reason to play with friends or react to other players? |
| Accessibility | Is the experience understandable on mobile and with low effects? |
| Trust | Does the game explain rewards, purchases, and updates honestly? |

Do not ship a low score by adding more content. Fix the underlying weakness first.
